# 焚境 · Story Bible (dark remake)

> Supersedes `docs/story.md` for the Unreal remake only. The web game keeps the old bible. Everything in §1-3 of the
> old bible that is not contradicted here still holds (the Abyssfire, the ley lines, the six keepers, the Heartflame,
> the brand, the prophecy, the twist, the ending, the length limits).

## 0. In one paragraph

The world was forged in the Abyssfire (渊火). Six keepers swore to tend it; the brightest, Ignaroth, feared most that
it would go out, burned his oath and tried to throw the world back into the fire to recast it as a world that never
grows cold and where no one is forgotten. The other five bound him under five seals, at the cost of their lives, and
chiselled away his name. For a thousand years he could only speak. Nine hundred years ago a sun-priest heard him in a
pyre and wrote down what the voice said; that scripture became the **Church of the Kept Flame** (守焰教会), which
reveres the five sealed flames without knowing whose voice founded it. This autumn the church preached the **Ashen
Crusade** (灰誓圣战) east to "hold the old stones". It broke on the **Red Field** (赤幡原). The Lord-Marshal ordered
the army to hold to the last man and executed deserters on an ancient stone dais, which was the Seal of Veins. The blood
cracked it. Now the slain army keeps its last order, the breach bleeds black fire, and the hero climbs out of a burn pit
outside **Knellwatch** (丧钟堡) with the brand on their palm. Across five lands the hero follows the crusade's road
backwards to the church's birthplace and down into the Abyss, relighting five seals that are, in truth, the five parts
of Ignaroth's key, and at the end gives the Heartflame back instead of letting it be taken.

**Theme line:** 火被夺取则焚世，被交还则暖世；誓言被焚则成灰，被守则成锁。 Seized, the fire burns; given, it warms. A
burned oath is ash; a kept oath is a lock, and sometimes a cage.

## 1. The world: 焚境 / the Burning March

- **The Abyssfire and the ley lines** are unchanged: the fire is the world's hearth, not evil; its warmth runs through
  the ley lines (灵脉).
- **The March** is the name the survivors give to the border lands the crusade crossed: burned hamlets, mass graves,
  abandoned siege lines, roads lined with helmets on spears. Each of the five chapters is a land on the crusade's road;
  the church's crimes are found wherever the seals are, which is why the holy war ties the five lands together.
- **Geography as descent.** The hero walks the road backwards: from the battlefield where the crusade died (Ch1), through
  the woods its witch-hunters hanged (Ch2), the mountain forges it chained (Ch3), the salt pyres where its faith was
  born (Ch4), into the wound where its last host went down (Ch5). Each land is darker and older than the one before.

## 3. The corruption

The Abyssfire seized and turned inside out. It reaches people in three layers; each has a mechanic and a look.

| Layer | What it is | What it does to people | Mechanic | Look (ash mode default) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **渊血 Breach-blood** | Cold black ichor welling from the breach, with violet sheen and drifting sparks | Whatever it touches goes grey, stops feeling pain, and becomes hungry. The wounded who touch it stop crying out; Mother Marl says that is the worst of it | Breach-blood monsters (clots, bloats); the **Ashnumb** status (麻灰: less healing received; slowed at full stacks) | Tar-black translucent shells, violet rims, embers inside, ash flakes when they burst |
| **誓缚 Oath-bound** | Ignaroth burned his own oath; his power makes everyone else's eternal | The dead who swore "hold the line" keep holding it. The Red-Oath zealots swore their blood to the Marshal. The deserters who broke their oath are hunted by it | The Unburied keep their last orders (formations, sentries, raise-again); zealots frenzy; deserters break and flee | The **oath-scar**: a closed fist crushing a flame, the church's open-palm sigil twisted, burned crimson into skin, armour and banners |
| **名与忘 Names and forgetting** | The villain is the one whose name was chiselled away; the church's promise is that no one is forgotten | Knellwatch tolls its bell once for every dead name it can count. A body without a name-tag cannot be tolled for. Thieves who cut tags off the dead steal more than silver | The bell motif in every main turn-in; the Tag-Thief quest; the Name Wall; the Lanternwisp | Name-tags (bronze discs on cords), the Name Wall of nailed tags, the cracked bell |

**The image that holds it together:** the church's sigil is an open palm cradling a flame; the zealots' oath-scar is
a fist crushing it; the hero's brand sits on an open palm, and the game ends with the hero opening that palm to give the
fire back.

## 5. Antagonists

- **Ignaroth the Oathburner (焚誓者·伊格纳罗斯).** Unchanged in motive and ending. In this remake he is heard only as a
  whisper (低语) until Chapter 5: short, gentle, certain, never shouting. He calls the hero 孩子 (child) and the seals 灯
  (lamps). The church worships his voice without knowing whose it is. He never lies about a price, only about who pays.
- **The Hierarch (守焰圣主).** The church's founder, kept alive for nine hundred years; the bible's void herald, the first
  to sign. His seal is on every crusade order the hero finds. He leads the crusade's last host into the rift and waits at
  the bottom (Chapter 5 mini-boss).

## 6. The hero

- **焚坑中醒来者, "the one from the pit".** The hero climbs out of the crusade's burn pit outside Knellwatch, covered
  in ash, with the brand and no memory. Crusader, deserter, or one of the executed? The game never says. NPCs speculate;
  the zealots call the hero 逃兵 (deserter); the Captain says the pit held 「所有人」 (everyone).
- **Why there.** The crusade dug its burn pit over a forgotten keepers' shrine on the plains' ley hub, where Aethelyn
  hid the Heartflame. When the hero's body went into the fire, the flame below rose and took them. The bottom of the Pit
  of the Faithful (chapter1.md §9) shows the empty niche, ringed by five burn marks, where it waited a thousand years.
- **The brand and the prophecy** stay word for word (the chaplain's order kept a rubbing of the keepers' tablet):
  「渊火再燃之夜，灰烬中将有人醒来。其身负五焰，其心藏一空。」「他将行过五地，重燃五印。开门者是他，闭门者亦是他。」
- **Voice.** The hero speaks rarely and briefly: 「你是谁？」「仗打完了。」「这火，不是你的。」「我知道。」
- **Arc.** From a body in a pit, counted among the dead, to the one who refuses to be anyone's lamp. The ending is
  unchanged: the hero gives the Heartflame back and becomes the new Flamekeeper (守火人).

## 7. The five-chapter descent

Each chapter: one hub at the edge of the corrupted land, a gradient from the familiar to the corrupted heart, one
descent, one act boss built up in four beats (named early; shown through victims and orders; a vision; the boss intro),
one mercy-or-duty dilemma, one trusted person who turns, one flame on the brand, and one ally who walks to the Ember
Tower. Zone ids are internal keys; display names change.

### Chapter 1 · 灰烬中醒来 · 赤幡原 / Woken from Ash · The Red Field (`emerald_plains`, Lv 1-10)

- **Place.** The battlefield where the crusade died; burned hamlets, burial trenches, a broken redoubt, the Red Banner
  on a mound of shields, the Breach Rim where the Seal of Veins lies cracked. Hub: **Knellwatch**, a toll-fort built
  around the keepers' bell. Second camp: **Crowmarket** (鸦集), the Gleaners.
- **Threat.** The Unburied hold their last order; the Gravegnawers eat the dead before they can be named; breach-blood
  wells up; the Red-Oath and deserters prey on the living.
- **Act boss.** Lord-Marshal Valdras at the Breach Rim, his scorched banner grown through his spine. Mini-boss: the
  Corpseherd. Descent: the Pit of the Faithful under the burn pit (optional cleanse).
- **The turn.** The Captain's confession at Ashwheat: 「桥是我烧的。他们在对岸喊，我听见了。我还是烧了。」 Then Valdras,
  dying: 「……是谁……下的撤退令？」 Each of them gave an order that killed people; only one of them is still standing.
- **Dilemma.** Corran's bounty on his own sergeant: whose hand does the head go to, his or the Captain's?
- **Betrayal seed.** Brother Aldwin hears the voice in the bell and calls it grace.
- **Flame.** The first flame (emerald) lights when the hero lays the branded palm on the bell at dawn; every risen dead
  on the field lies down at once. Ally to the tower: Mother Marl (herb garden).

### Chapter 2 · 被抹去的名字 · 缢林 / The Erased Name · The Hanging Wood (`twilight_forest`, Lv 10-20)

- **Place.** The moon-oaks where the Lamp-Bearers hanged Selenne's priestesses; the wood stopped letting its dead go.
  Hub: **the Hollow Mill** (空磨坊), refugees around one fire. Descent: the Root Barrow beneath the gallows-oak.
- **Threat.** The folk rule of the wood, 「吊死鬼找替身」: the hanged cannot come down until someone takes their place,
  so they drop on travellers. The Moon-Sick (villagers mid-turn), bog-drowned dead, Lamp-Bearer remnants with lanterns
  and nets. Hanged figures are always executed victims, shown as shrouded, rope-bound bundles; never self-harm.
- **Act boss.** Volgan the Moon-Eater, the sacred wolf fed corpses beneath the gallows until he ate the moon. His death
  frees his cub (`pet_storm_wolf`, `cs_tf_moonfang` kept).
- **The turn.** `cs_tf_hermit` is kept: there were six sages, not five, and the church's Voice of the Flame is the
  erased sixth.
- **Betrayal.** Brother Aldwin came east "to hear the Voice clearly" and became the Cantor of the Hanged (mini-boss,
  the `shadow_weaver` slot). He raises the hanged: the Chapter 1 raiser lesson, now personal.
- **Dilemma.** Gret the Rope-Cutter (the scout, re-cast) cuts the hanged down at night. Her brother still hangs: cut him
  down (and he will need a substitute) or leave him.
- **Flame.** Silver. Ally to the tower: Gret.

### Chapter 3 · 命运之锤 · 锁砧山 / The Hammer of Fate · The Chained Anvil (`anvil_mountains`, Lv 20-30)

- **Place.** The deep forges the crusade requisitioned; the smiths were chained to their anvils "until the war is won".
  The war is lost and they are still working, dead. Hub: **the Last Gate** (末门), a gatehouse barricaded from inside.
  Descent: the Deep Shift (`sub_dwarf_mine`).
- **Threat.** The Chained (dead smiths and miners with hammers and chain-flails), Oathstone gargoyles that hunt anyone
  leaving the mountain, slag golems forged from slag and the dead.
- **Act boss.** Gorm on the empty throne, wearing the dead king's chains; the whisper lives in the chains. Mini-boss:
  the Iron Guardian, still enforcing the last order (shield-block AI).
- **The turn.** The Hammer of Fate is reforged: 「锤能锻印，也能锻钥匙。」 The villain admits he tempted the high priest.
- **Dilemma.** The dwarf elder sealed the chained smiths in to save the city. Free them (and break the oath that keeps
  the forge, and the seal, burning) or keep the forge.
- **Flame.** Red. Ally to the tower: a freed smith.

### Chapter 4 · 日冕余烬 · 盐葬海 / Embers of the Sun Crown · The Salt Pyre (`scorching_desert`, Lv 30-40)

- **Place.** The Sun Crown's salt flats, where the church was born. Offerings were chosen by lot (抽签) and burned on salt
  pyres to "feed the flame"; the sun-bird Helia is bound to the great pyre, burning but never dying. Hub: **the Salt
  Well** (盐井), nomads in a dry cistern who descend from the sacrificers. Descent: the salt tombs.
- **Threat.** The Embalmed (salt-statued dead; never exposed bone), pyre-souls of the sacrificed (the `fire_elemental`
  slot, burn), salt scorpions, the road-eating worm, living sun-cultists.
- **Act boss.** Helia, chained; tragic, not evil. Freed, she grants `pet_phoenix`. Mini-boss: the Deathless High Priest
  (the `sand_wraith` slot), who broke the Sun Seal.
- **The turn.** The church's founding scripture is found in the high priest's tomb, in his hand: the Voice of the Flame
  was Ignaroth. The empty heart of the brand aches for the first time.
- **Betrayal.** The nomad elder still draws lots.
- **Flame.** Gold. Ally to the tower: Helia (as a pet) and the water-diviner.

### Chapter 5 · 渊火 · 渊之创口 / Abyssfire · The Open Wound (`abyss_rift`, Lv 40-50)

- **Place.** The rift where the Hierarch led the crusade's last host "to bring the flame home". They were remade into
  breach-knights. Hub: **the Warden's Vigil** (守望哨). Descent: the furnace altar (`sub_demon_altar`).
- **Threat.** Breach-knights (crusaders fused with abyssal plate), rift-grubs (burrowing swarmers that burst), riftborn
  brutes, the Keening (eyeless robed wailing spirits, deliberately non-sexualised).
- **Act boss.** Ignaroth, Abyssfire Incarnate (`demon_lord`); his hellfire, dark nova and summons become real abilities.
  Mini-boss: the Hierarch.
- **The turn.** `cs_ar_gate` is kept: the seals' souls are lock and key; the hero has been carrying the key.
- **Ending.** Unchanged: the hero gives the Heartflame back. Across the five lands every oath-bound dead lies down at
  once, the Chapter 1 finale image now for the whole world. The Captain's last line in the epilogue: 「我替你敲了一下钟。
  不为死人，为你。」 (I rang the bell once for you. Not for the dead. For you.)

## 8. Endgame, refuge, pets

- **The Abyss Labyrinth → 无底阶 / the Bottomless Stair.** Endless floors as memories of the five fields (the crypt floor
  reuses the Pit kit); curses are re-voiced as oaths (`volatile` becomes 焚誓 Pyre-Oath and finally uses the on-death
  burst). 卡萨诺尔 (Kasanor) still waits below. Data unchanged until the Labyrinth runtime exists.
- **The Ember Tower (余烬之塔)** stays the one warm place in the game: 「那里的火，不要人偿。」 (Its fire asks no one to
  pay.) Each chapter's ally walks there carrying a little of their hub's light. Residents are scarred, not cured.
- **Ley-beasts** are re-toned gently, never darkened. Chapter 1's `pet_sprite` becomes 灯魂 / the Lanternwisp: the
  lantern of Pell, the youngest of the burial detail, still walking grave to grave.

## 11. Voice guide

### Shared rules

- **Short and tired.** NPC lines are one or two sentences. Survivors do not explain; they count, they order, they
  apologise to the dead. No jokes outside the hub, and very few inside it.
- **Concrete nouns.** Names, rations, lanterns, tags, bridges, bells, ash. Never 「邪恶」「黑暗的力量」 / "evil", "dark
  forces". Say what was done, by whom, and what it cost.
- **Quests are pleas, orders or dying words**, often with a body present. Completions are bittersweet: something is
  saved and something is lost in the same line.
- **The whisper** is gentle and certain. It never threatens, never shouts, and is always partly right.
- **The hero** speaks rarely; when they do, it is short and plain.
- **Limits** (zh code points, enforced by `quests_test`): dialogue / whisper ≤ 60, narration ≤ 70, slide ≤ 110, title
  ≤ 12, epithet ≤ 14, quest offer ≤ 70, complete ≤ 50; quest progress lines (new) ≤ 50.

### zh-CN

- Short clauses, full-width punctuation, the ellipsis 「……」 for a breath that will not finish, the dash 「——」 for an
  order cut off. Four-character phrases sparingly (one per line at most).
- Military register where it fits (军令、死守、撤退令、军饷、点卯), church register for Aldwin (经、圣火、名字), plain speech
  for everyone else.
- Avoid translationese (「被……所……」, chains of 「的」, 「他们的他们」), internet slang, archaic 文言 beyond the prophecy and
  the catechism.
- Names: two syllables where possible (瑟琳、奥德温、玛勒、柯兰); roles stand in for names in barks (队长、神父、军医).

### English

- Plain, mostly Anglo-Saxon words; contractions are fine; no thee / thou; no quips, no heroic one-liners.
- Translate meaning and rhythm, not words: keep the line as short as the Chinese, keep the pause where the 「……」 is.
- Keep proper nouns consistent with the glossary (Knellwatch, the Red Field, the Unburied, the Breach).

## 12. What to avoid

- **Names and words** never used: Diablo, Sanctuary, Tristram, Deckard Cain, Leoric / Skeleton King, Lachdanan, Lazarus,
  Butcher, Fallen, Lilith, Andariel, Duriel, Mephisto, Baal, Belial, Horadric / Horadrim, Zakarum, Nephalem, Worldstone,
  Rogue Encampment, Kashya, Akara, Ogden, Griswold, Adria, Wirt, Blood Raven, Helltide; Path of Exile's Wraeclast and
  "Exile"; Grim Dawn's Cairn, Devil's Crossing, Aetherial, Chthonian; Last Epoch's Eterra; Diablo IV's "the Drowned",
  "Coven" and Nevesk. Catchphrases: "Stay awhile and listen", "Fresh meat".
- **Iconic designs** never used: a horned red demon lord; a cleaver-and-apron brute in a larder of hooked or spiked
  corpses; small red imps with a resurrecting shaman as one package; a transmute cube; named rune sets and runewords; the
  Tristram melody or a 12-string town theme; twin life / mana globes; a cathedral descent copied level for level; a
  crowned skeleton king on a throne in a crypt. Valdras has no crown, no throne, no crypt and no skull face; his adds come
  from a standard-bearer, not from him. The Corpseherd is a human with a bell crook and raises human dead; the
  Gravegnawers never travel with her.
- **Sensitive material.** No plague, quarantine or lockdown imagery: no doors nailed shut from outside, no cordons
  keeping people in, no "we are not sick" pleas (Ashwheat's doors are barred from inside by the villagers themselves).
  No torture on screen, no sexual violence, no sexualised enemy designs. Children are never monsters and never on-screen
  victims (Pell is remembered through his lantern). Executions are aftermath (an echo, a dais, a journal), never shown in
  progress; hanging is always an execution by others, never self-harm. The church is fictional: no crosses, crescents,
  Templar crosses, real saints or real places; its faithful are victims as often as villains, and the corrupting agent is
  Ignaroth, not faith.
- **Gore.** Ash mode by default (DR6). Breach-blood reads as tar and ash; corpses crumble to ash husks; no exposed
  skeletons or skulls in Chapter 1 (the Gallowbones of Chapter 2 and the Embalmed of Chapter 4 get rope-bound or salt
  variants). The optional gore layer adds blood decals and persistent corpses only.
